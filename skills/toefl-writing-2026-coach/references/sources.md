## Official ETS

- Writing scoring guides: https://www.ets.org/content/dam/ets-org/pdfs/toefl/writing-rubrics.pdf
  - Separate holistic `0–5` guides for Write an Email and Academic Discussion.
- TOEFL iBT 2026 specifications: https://www.ets.org/content/dam/ets-india/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-test-specifications-2026.pdf
  - Ten Build a Sentence items at one point each; Email and Academic Discussion at five points each; Writing is linear; published base structure is 12 items and about 23 minutes.
- Current Writing overview: https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/content/writing/.html
  - Academic Discussion asks the test taker to state and support an opinion, develop ideas, respond to viewpoints, and use an academic tone.

## User-provided discussion-complete response note

- Received as pasted text on 2026-10-12; authorship and original publication context were not verified.
- Useful strategy: answer the professor directly, connect to one student's claim, add a new mechanism with support, use that development to address the second student's concern, then refine the position with a limitation and concise conclusion.
- Reusable map: `Professor → Student 1 → new idea → example/consequence → Student 2 → nuance → conclusion`.
- Confidence: strong coaching structure when both student posts contain distinct usable ideas. It is not an independent ETS scoring checklist, and mentioning both students mechanically does not replace relevance, development, clarity, range, or accuracy.

## User-provided five-sentence disagreement scaffold

- Received as pasted text on 2026-10-12; authorship and original publication context were not verified.
- Useful contribution: a compact path from respectful acknowledgment to a clear position, original concern, reasoned response to a classmate, concrete example, and inference tied to the professor's question.
- Editorial treatment: combined courtesy and stance to reduce filler; corrected possessives, punctuation, subject–verb agreement, and the unpaired transition `Second of all`; replaced `he/she` with a name or singular `they`; and reframed “contradict” as a reasoned counterpoint.
