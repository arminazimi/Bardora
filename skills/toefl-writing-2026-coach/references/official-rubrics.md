# Official scoring reference

This file paraphrases ETS material for operational use. The official links are listed in `sources.md`.

## 2026 Writing structure

- Ten Build a Sentence items, each worth a maximum of 1 raw point.
- One Write an Email response, worth a maximum of 5 raw points.
- One Write for an Academic Discussion response, worth a maximum of 5 raw points.
- The Writing section is linear, not adaptive. The published base structure is 12 items and approximately 23 minutes.
- ETS reports the section on a 1–6 scale in half-band increments. Do not claim an exact raw-score conversion because ETS has not published a simple conversion table.

## Build a Sentence

Award `1` when the arranged words or phrases form the intended complete, grammatical sentence or question; otherwise award `0`. Diagnose word order, clause structure, auxiliaries, agreement, verb form, modifier placement, articles, and prepositions.

## Write an Email 0–5

Score the response holistically across communicative purpose, elaboration, language range, accuracy, and social conventions.

### 5 — fully successful

The email is effective and clearly expressed. It develops the requested purpose well, uses varied syntax and precise natural wording, consistently fits the audience and action, and contains almost no errors beyond normal timed-writing slips.

### 4 — generally successful

The email is mostly effective and easy to understand. It provides adequate support, shows syntactic variety and appropriate wording, generally maintains suitable politeness, register, organization, and request formulation, and has few errors.

### 3 — partially successful

The email generally completes the task, but parts may be less clear or effective. Development only partly supports the purpose; range is moderate; noticeable errors or convention problems occur.

### 2 — mostly unsuccessful

The response attempts the task but is limited, partly irrelevant, or hard to interpret. Connected sentences exist, but syntax and vocabulary are narrow and errors accumulate.

### 1 — unsuccessful

The response is extremely limited or largely unintelligible, with little development, fragmented language, severe frequent errors, or minimal original language.

### 0

Blank, not in English, rejects the task, wholly copied, wholly unrelated, or arbitrary keystrokes.

## Academic Discussion 0–5

Score the response holistically across relevance and development, syntactic and lexical range, accuracy, clarity, and original contribution.

### 5 — fully successful

The contribution is highly relevant and very clear. Explanations, examples, or details are well developed; structures are effectively varied; wording is precise and natural; almost no errors occur beyond normal timed-writing slips.

### 4 — generally successful

The contribution is relevant and easy to understand. Explanations, examples, or details are adequately developed; structures show variety; wording is appropriate; few errors occur.

### 3 — partially successful

The contribution is mostly relevant and understandable. Some explanation, example, or detail is missing, unclear, or irrelevant; range is present but limited; noticeable language errors occur.

### 2 — mostly unsuccessful

The response attempts to contribute, but ideas may be poorly developed or only partly relevant. Syntax and vocabulary are limited and accumulated errors make ideas hard to follow.

### 1 — unsuccessful

Only fragments of a relevant attempt remain, with few coherent ideas, severely limited language, serious frequent errors, or mostly borrowed wording.

### 0

Blank, not in English, rejects the task, wholly copied, wholly unrelated, or arbitrary keystrokes.

## Calibration rules

- Grade communication and development, not perfection. A few typos or local grammar errors do not automatically push a clear developed response below 4.
- A 5 still requires strong development and consistently controlled language; advanced-looking vocabulary cannot compensate for vague support or inaccurate usage.
- A response can score well without a conclusion, a counterargument, or an explicit reference to a named student because none is an independent ETS rubric requirement.
- Treat templates as scaffolding. Judge whether the actual response addresses the prompt with original, relevant language.
