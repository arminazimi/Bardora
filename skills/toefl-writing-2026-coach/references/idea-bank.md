# Idea bank for fast development

Use this reference to generate a relevant reason, not to paste a memorized paragraph. The response still needs to answer the exact prompt.

## Fast workflow

1. Identify the decision in the question and the position being defended.
2. Choose one dimension with a direct causal link to that position.
3. Develop it as `claim → mechanism → specific example → consequence`.
4. Replace absolute claims with realistic language such as `can`, `may`, `is likely to`, or `makes it easier to`.
5. Keep the example plausible and closely tied to the reason.

## Deep 10-lens brainstorm mode

Use this mode only when the learner explicitly asks for a deep idea bank, a ten-dimension analysis, or broad topic preparation. It is a prewriting tool, not a recommendation to put ten ideas into one timed response. For ordinary prompt preparation, offer only two or three strong routes.

Test the topic through these ten lenses:

| Lens | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| Economic / financial | How could the choice affect costs, income, jobs, investment, or the use of limited resources? |
| Time / efficiency | Could it save time, shorten a process, reduce unnecessary effort, or improve access? |
| Physical / mental health | Could it affect safety, stress, motivation, physical health, or general wellbeing? |
| Productivity / performance | Could it change the quality or quantity of academic or workplace output? |
| Environmental | Could it affect energy, waste, emissions, consumption, habitats, or resilience? |
| Cognitive / social skills | Could it build or weaken problem-solving, creativity, teamwork, communication, or independent judgment? |
| Ethics / civics | What duties, rights, fairness concerns, or effects on public trust are involved? |
| Academic / professional alignment | How does the issue connect to the learner's field, transferable skills, or future work? |
| Society / infrastructure | Could it affect institutions, public services, transport, technology, communities, or equal access? |
| Intergenerational equity | Could present choices create benefits, costs, or irreversible risks for future generations? |

For **each** lens, provide:

1. **Argument:** one distinct claim that directly answers the prompt.
2. **Mechanism:** the causal link explaining why the claim could be true.
3. **Concession–contrast sentence:** one natural sentence using a structure such as `Although`, `Even if`, or `While` and expressing a genuine limitation or competing consideration.
4. **Realistic example:** a concise situation with one concrete detail and no invented research, quotation, or statistic.
5. **Topic language:** two or three controllable collocations that fit this exact argument.

After generating the ten lenses:

1. Remove arguments that merely repeat the same mechanism under different labels.
2. Mark a lens **limited fit** when its connection to the prompt is weak; do not invent relevance to complete the table.
3. Rank the best three routes using prompt relevance, ease of development, distinctiveness, and the learner's ability to express them accurately.
4. Label one route **best fit** and expand it as `claim → mechanism → example → consequence`.
5. If the learner has provided a field of study, personalize the academic/professional lens. If not, use a general study-and-career connection and note that it can be personalized later without blocking the exercise.

### Deep-mode language controls

- A concession must create real tension: `Although public campaigns can be expensive, they may reduce long-term cleanup costs by changing consumer behavior.` It must not be an empty `Although` clause that repeats the main position.
- Prefer precise, reusable language over ornamental vocabulary. `reduce operating costs` is stronger and safer than an obscure synonym used incorrectly.
- Keep one main idea per lens and make the example demonstrate its mechanism.
- Use calibrated claims such as `can`, `may`, `is likely to`, and `could`, unless the outcome is certain.
- In 2026 coaching, describe the target as a high-band `0–5` task response or a strong reported `1–6` Writing performance. Do not present the legacy `25+` scale as the current score system.

## Reusable examples

Adapt the mechanism and details rather than copying the wording.

- **Technology:** automation reduces repetitive work, allowing employees to spend more time on creative or customer-facing tasks.
- **Public transportation:** lower travel costs and fewer private-car trips can benefit both household budgets and air quality.
- **University resources:** better laboratories and library access can improve research quality and create stronger learning opportunities.
- **Second-language learning:** a durable communication skill can expand later study, travel, and employment options.
- **Extracurricular activities:** team roles give students repeated practice in communication, responsibility, and handling mistakes.
- **Digital books:** immediate access can save search time and help students obtain materials when libraries are closed.

## Language frames

```text
[Action] can improve [outcome] because [mechanism].
This matters because [specific consequence].
For example, [plausible situation with one concrete detail].
As a result, [result that directly supports the position].
```

For comparison questions:

```text
[Option A] is more [efficient/accessible/economical] than [Option B] because [specific difference].
For instance, [concrete comparison].
Therefore, [relevant consequence].
```

## Quality controls

- Use a reason only when it genuinely fits the prompt; generic relevance is not enough.
- Prefer one developed reason to several labels from the bank.
- Present personal examples as illustrations, not proof of a universal rule.
- Use evidence-like details without inventing statistics, studies, or quotations.
- Soften predictions. Education can improve employment prospects; it does not guarantee a good job.
- Use `ensure` or `guarantee` only when the outcome is truly certain.
- Avoid speculative claims about celebrities' emotions and unsupported neuroscience claims.
- Prefer natural forms: `make an effort`, `help conserve the environment`, `gain independence`, `relieve stress`, `an alternative to`, and `more accessible than`.
- Skip decorative proverbs and generic statements when a prompt-specific mechanism would add more value.
