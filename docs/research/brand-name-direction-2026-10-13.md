# Brand name and identity direction — 2026-10-13

## Purpose and limits

This note evaluates a public master brand for the open-source AI English-test-preparation skill and its later learner, educator, and institutional products. It uses first-party product pages, official trademark guidance, and registry RDAP responses. It is naming and product strategy, not legal advice or a trademark clearance opinion.

## Executive recommendation

Do **not** use **Shakespeare** as the exact master brand. Keep Shakespeare as a creative reference—language craft, confidence, memorable expression—but choose a distinctive name that the project can own in search, software marketplaces, domains, and future product categories.

The strongest low-collision working direction is **Fluencraft**:

- It retains the best part of the Shakespeare idea—language as a craft—without using a famous person's name or the legally busier word “Bard.”
- It can cover Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing, study planning, teacher tools, and later additional English tests.
- It is suggestive rather than purely descriptive: learners can infer fluency and deliberate practice, while the project can build distinctive search equity around the compound.
- At the time of the RDAP snapshot, neither `fluencraft.com` nor `fluencraft.ai` had a registration record. This is not a guarantee that either domain can be purchased.

**Eloqora** is the strongest highly distinctive alternative. **Langwright** is the best alternative if the team wants a subtle playwright/Shakespeare echo, though the coined meaning needs explanation and the term has unrelated fan-community use. **LinguaBard** is the closest emotional translation of the founder's Shakespeare preference, but it should not be adopted without heightened legal review. Google's official trademark list still identifies **Bard™** as its generative-AI chatbot mark, even though the product was renamed, and the proposed product is also AI software. A compound such as LinguaBard may ultimately be clearable, but the overlap is material enough that it should not be treated as the default. [Google trademark list](https://about.google/brand-resource-center/trademark-list/)

Recommended working lockup:

> **Fluencraft**  
> Open AI coach for academic English and test readiness

Use the TOEFL® mark only in secondary informational copy, for example “Independent preparation for the TOEFL® test,” and display the required non-endorsement notice. ETS says its marks, translations, and transliterations may not be used in company names, product names, domain names, social usernames, website identifiers, trademark applications, or advertising keywords. [ETS trademark guidelines](https://www.ets.org/legal/trademarks.html)

Before adopting any candidate, run exact, phonetic, and confusing-similarity searches in the intended jurisdictions and classes, then register the domains and primary social handles on the same day. If the product will be commercialized, obtain professional trademark advice.

## Evaluation of “Shakespeare”

### What works

- **Immediate recognition:** the name strongly signals English, literature, language mastery, and cultural authority.
- **Emotional character:** it can support a mentor-like personality and a richer identity than a generic test-preparation name.
- **Category expansion:** it is not tied to one exam, so it could theoretically extend into broader English learning.

### What does not work

1. **The expected product is wrong.** “Shakespeare” primarily suggests plays, literature, creative writing, archaic English, or writing generation. The actual product teaches modern academic English across four skills and measures test readiness. The name therefore creates an explanation burden and may intimidate learners who already lack confidence.
2. **Search ownership is poor.** Branded searches must compete with the author, schools, cultural institutions, study resources, and existing software. Exact and near-exact commercial uses already include a [Shakespeare AI copywriting app on Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/product-description-writer-1), the [Shakespeare.ai writing product](https://app.shakespeare.ai/authentication/free-trial), and an [education app called Shakespeare AI in Apple’s App Store](https://apps.apple.com/nz/app/shakespeare-ai/id6763808871). This makes it difficult to build a clean search result page around the new product.
3. **The strongest domains are occupied.** The authoritative `.com` and `.ai` RDAP services returned registration records for [shakespeare.com](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/shakespeare.com) and [shakespeare.ai](https://rdap.identitydigital.services/rdap/domain/shakespeare.ai) on 2026-10-13.
4. **It is comparatively hard to spell.** The 11-character name contains several spelling traps for international learners. USPTO naming guidance specifically recommends considering whether the public can remember, pronounce, and spell a proposed mark. [USPTO: Strong trademarks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks)
5. **The commercial namespace is crowded.** The existence of multiple education and AI-writing uses does not by itself prove infringement or prevent registration, but it raises clearance cost, confusion risk, marketplace ambiguity, and the chance that users assume the product is about Shakespeare.
6. **The cultural frame is narrower than the mission.** Shakespeare can convey prestige, but a worldwide learner ecosystem should not look like an Elizabethan-literature course or depend on one Western cultural figure. This is a positioning inference, not a legal conclusion.

### Conclusion on Shakespeare

Use **Shakespeare as inspiration, not identity**. The product can borrow the ideas of mastery, voice, craft, rehearsal, and performance while using an ownable master brand. A future campaign, mascot, lesson series, or public-domain literary content pack could reference Shakespeare only after appropriate review; the platform itself should not be named after him.

| Product | First-party naming pattern | Strategic lesson |

| [Magoosh TOEFL](https://toefl.magoosh.com/) | Distinctive invented master brand plus the exam as a product descriptor; licensing and official content claims are separated and explicit. | A coined master brand can become memorable while supporting multiple exam lines. |
| [TestGlider](https://www.testglider.com/en) | Suggestive benefit metaphor; the same brand supports TOEFL, IELTS, PTE, vocabulary, skill building, and community. Its footer separately states trademark and endorsement status. | A broad master brand is more extensible than an exam-specific name. |
| [BestMyTest TOEFL](https://www.bestmytest.com/toefl) | Highly descriptive, outcome-oriented name with immediate category clarity. | Descriptive names are easy to understand but harder to distinguish and protect. |
| [E2 Test Prep](https://www.e2language.com/) | Short abstract master brand plus a descriptive category phrase; supports several English-proficiency exams and general English products. | A compact master brand can span consumer, tutor, and multi-exam offerings. |
| [Duolingo English Test preparation](https://englishtest.duolingo.com/prepare) | A highly distinctive existing master brand extended into a test and its preparation experience. | Long-term brand equity comes from owning a name, not from putting every category keyword inside it. |

The consistent pattern is an independent master brand followed by a clear descriptor. The project should therefore optimize the master brand for distinctiveness, memorability, and extensibility, then use plain-language subtitles and landing-page copy to explain the category.

The USPTO describes fanciful, arbitrary, and suggestive marks as stronger than descriptive or generic marks and notes that stronger marks are easier to distinguish and defend. [USPTO: Strong trademarks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks)

### Recommended validation round

Test **Fluencraft**, **Eloqora**, and **Langwright** with at least 15–20 target learners from multiple first-language backgrounds and a small educator panel. This deliberately compares an understandable craft-led name, a highly ownable coined name, and a subtle playwright-inspired name without the `Bard` conflict. Ask each participant, before showing the product description:

1. What do you think this product does?
2. How would you pronounce the name?
3. How would you spell it after hearing it once?
4. Which name feels most trustworthy for high-stakes test preparation?
5. Which name could credibly become a larger learning platform?

Measure unaided spelling, pronunciation agreement, perceived purpose, trust, and preference. Do not select from preference alone; a name that receives fewer votes but has much better spelling and purpose recognition may be the stronger global brand.

## Brand and theme direction

### Positioning

The brand should feel like a **serious modern learning companion**, not a novelty chatbot and not a traditional prep-book publisher. Its authority should come from visible practice, evidence-backed feedback, transparent limitations, and learner progress.

Recommended brand promise:

> Practice deliberately. Understand the feedback. Prove the improvement.

Recommended personality:

- intelligent without sounding academic or distant;
- encouraging without childish gamification;
- precise about evidence and uncertainty;
- international rather than tied to one accent or culture;
- literary in craft, modern in execution.

### Visual system

Use a contemporary editorial system rather than literal Shakespeare imagery:

| Role | Direction | Suggested starting value |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Midnight ink / deep navy for trust and focus | `#161A2B` |
| Canvas | Warm paper / ivory for reading comfort | `#F7F2E8` |
| Heritage accent | Copper for craft and human warmth; use decoratively, not for body text | `#C57B3A` |
| Digital accent | Deep teal for accessible actions and progress | `#0F766E` |
| Typography | Contemporary editorial serif for headings, highly readable neutral sans for interface and long-form text | Select open, multilingual families after Persian and Latin rendering tests |

The logo should be an abstract, scalable mark that can work as a plugin icon: a speech path, progress path, or waveform that subtly resolves into a quill or open page. It should remain recognizable at 16–32 pixels and in one color. Ink on warm paper, white on deep teal, and deep teal on warm paper meet the WCAG 2.2 AA 4.5:1 contrast threshold for normal text; copper should remain a non-text accent. [W3C contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)

Avoid:

- portraits or silhouettes of Shakespeare;
- Tudor costumes, theatre masks, wax seals, parchment textures, and novelty calligraphy;
- black-and-gold “luxury” styling that reduces accessibility;
- brains, robot heads, graduation caps, or generic AI sparkles;
- British-national imagery, because the target is global academic English rather than cultural imitation.

### Product naming architecture

Use one master brand and descriptive modules:

- **Fluencraft Skill** — free open-source agent skill
- **Fluencraft Practice** — learner application
- **Fluencraft Pro** — paid learner service
- **Fluencraft Classroom** — teacher and cohort tools
- **Fluencraft Institute** — institutional administration, if needed later

Do not put `TOEFL` inside the master brand, repository owner, package identifier, plugin identifier, domain, or social handle. A public page can use an informational title such as “FLUENCRAFT PREPARATION FOR THE TOEFL® TEST” only if it complies with the current ETS rules, uses the appropriate symbol and generic term, keeps the independent brand prominent, and includes the required non-endorsement notice. Have public packaging reviewed before distribution where required. [ETS trademark guidelines](https://www.ets.org/legal/trademarks.html)

## Trademark and legal follow-up

Domain availability, a search-engine check, and the absence of an obvious exact match do **not** establish trademark availability.

- The USPTO says a clearance search must consider marks that look alike, sound alike, have similar meanings, or create similar commercial impressions, as well as whether the goods and services are related. It also warns that a comprehensive search can be complex. [USPTO: Federal trademark searching](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching)
- WIPO’s Global Brand Database covers international and participating national/regional collections but advises searching national and regional registers as well. [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database/index), [WIPO availability guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/check-availability)
- EUIPO says TMview covers EU national offices, EUIPO, and many outside offices, but refining a search reduces rather than eliminates risk. [EUIPO trademark availability guidance](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/trade-marks/before-applying/availability), [EUIPO eSearch](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/)
- Registrability varies by jurisdiction, class, existing use, pronunciation, translation, and related services. Unregistered prior rights may also matter.

Recommended clearance sequence:

1. Keep the final three-name shortlist private.
2. Search exact, plural, joined/separated, misspelled, phonetic, and translated variants.
3. Search likely software and education goods/services and adjacent AI, tutoring, assessment, and publishing uses.
4. Check USPTO, EUIPO/TMview, WIPO, and priority national registers.
5. Check company registries, app stores, GitHub, package registries, social platforms, and ordinary web use.
6. Obtain professional review before accepting payments, signing institutional contracts, or filing.
7. Register the chosen `.com`, matching `.ai`, relevant handles, and defensive spelling variants together.

## Decision proposal

Advance these three names to learner testing and preliminary legal clearance:

1. **Fluencraft** — recommended master brand for the complete ecosystem and the lowest-collision strategic direction in this scan.
2. **Eloqora** — highly distinctive alternative if the team accepts teaching the pronunciation and meaning.
3. **Langwright** — Shakespeare-adjacent alternative through the idea of a language/playwright, without using `Shakespeare` or `Bard`.

If rapid validation confirms pronunciation, spelling, and purpose recognition, adopt **Fluencraft** with the modern editorial/craft visual direction above. Preserve “Shakespeare” as internal inspiration only. Do not adopt any Bard-formative name until a professional search has considered Google's live and pending rights in related AI software and services.
