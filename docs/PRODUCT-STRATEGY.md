# Product strategy: from open skill to global learning ecosystem

## Vision

Build the most trusted independent, open, and evidence-led learning ecosystem for people preparing for the current TOEFL iBT® format—useful to an individual learner on day one, dependable for teachers, portable across AI agents, and sustainable as a long-term platform.

The project identifies compatibility with the TOEFL test but is not an ETS product, official preparation provider, score service, or source of secure test content. An independent brand is required before broad public promotion.

The source-backed constraints and platform research behind this strategy are recorded in [Global ecosystem strategy research — October 12, 2026](research/ecosystem-strategy-2026-10-12.md). The research includes provisional time horizons; this operating strategy uses exit gates until team capacity and pilot cadence are known.

## Strategic thesis

The durable advantage is not an unlimited volume of generated questions. It is a compounding trust and learning system:

1. **Format truth:** current test facts have dated first-party sources and a response process when the format changes.
2. **Observable learning:** every attempt produces evidence, a priority, a targeted retry, and a measurable next check.
3. **Reproducible quality:** public behavior cases, regression results, modality limits, and calibration gaps are visible.
4. **Portable continuity:** the free core works without an account, while consented learner state can move between supported agents.
5. **Teacher and community judgment:** qualified educators review high-impact behavior and contributors can improve the system through governed pathways.
6. **Service-based monetization:** paid value comes from hosted convenience, computation, advanced workflows, collaboration, and support—not from weakening the free learning core.

## Product layers

### 1. Open learning core — free forever

- The portable Agent Skill and all 12 task-type coaching contracts.
- Original basic practice, evidence-backed feedback, retries, and diagnostic assembly.
- The learner-state schema, local/session-only use, and manual export/import.
- Public tests, evaluation fixtures, format-source audits, and core installation guides.
- Community translations and accessibility contracts that do not depend on a paid account.

The open core remains independently useful. A learner must not need the hosted product to read the instructions, run basic coaching, or retain a personal export.

### 2. Hosted learner product — free and Pro tiers

- Account-optional onboarding followed by explicit sync consent.
- Cross-device history, calendar-aware plans, reminders, and progress visualizations.
- Managed audio capture, transcription, pronunciation evidence, and higher-cost model workflows.
- Adaptive practice sequencing, full mock-test orchestration, and richer longitudinal analysis.
- Operational reliability, backups, export, deletion, abuse controls, and customer support.

The free hosted tier should demonstrate a complete improvement loop. Pro may increase convenience, limits, depth, computation, and continuity; it should not claim official scoring or sell access to copied test material.

### 3. Educator and institution product

- Teacher-authored assignments and approved original content packs.
- Cohorts, progress review, feedback queues, and learner consent controls.
- Classroom and organization administration, role-based access, audit history, and data-retention settings.
- Interoperability with common learning systems only after privacy and security requirements are defined.

### 4. Ecosystem platform

- A versioned extension contract for agent adapters, content packs, evaluators, and learning activities.
- A verified registry that distinguishes core-maintained, educator-reviewed, community, and commercial extensions.
- Developer documentation, compatibility tests, provenance, permissions, and revocation rules.
- Partner pathways for teachers, schools, researchers, accessibility contributors, and language communities.

## Sequenced growth plan

Progress is gate-based. Calendar estimates should be added only after team capacity and pilot cadence are known.

### Stage A — Trusted open-source v1.0

**Outcome:** a safe, independently branded release that works in at least two maintained agent environments.

- Complete human review of high-impact Writing and Speaking feedback.
- Finish cross-platform runners, adversarial integrity tests, accessibility review, privacy review, and IP review.
- Select the independent name, visual identity, domains, package identifiers, and migration plan.
- Publish installation, validation, security, governance, and release documentation.
- Add protected review rules, private vulnerability reporting, dependency and secret scanning, checksums, and artifact provenance for distributed bundles.
- Release signed artifacts with reproducible evaluation results.

**Gate:** zero known critical privacy, security, copyright, or trademark blockers; every maintained host passes the release suite.

### Stage B — Adoption and community v1.5

**Outcome:** learners and teachers can discover, trust, install, use, and improve the project without founder assistance.

- Launch a fast multilingual website with installation paths, live examples, limitations, and comparison by use case.
- Provide a one-command or guided installer for maintained hosts.
- Establish issue templates, contribution ladders, maintainer rules, moderation, a code of conduct, and a teacher advisory circle.
- Publish a transparent evaluation dashboard and a dated format-change watch.
- Create a localization framework with terminology review rather than uncontrolled file copying.
- Build ethical growth loops: shareable non-score progress summaries, teacher referrals, contributor recognition, and reproducible public benchmarks.

**Gate:** activation, repeat-use, learning, and trust metrics can be measured with consent; support and contribution workflows operate on documented service levels.

### Stage C — Hosted learner platform v2.0

**Outcome:** the open core is available through a polished service with reliable long-term learning workflows.

- Build web-first onboarding, account-optional trial use, consented sync, export, deletion, and recovery.
- Decide and document the age policy before account launch; complete the applicable child-privacy review before collecting personal data from minors.
- Add a real adaptive planner based on task-level evidence and review history.
- Add managed audio workflows with accent-fair evidence boundaries and human escalation paths.
- Orchestrate realistic full diagnostics and mock experiences using original content.
- Add accessible progress views, review reminders, cost controls, observability, and incident response.
- Instrument the improvement loop with privacy-preserving events and explicit analytics consent.

**Gate:** pilot evidence shows repeat learning value and acceptable reliability, privacy, safety, latency, and cost-to-serve before monetization expands.

### Stage D — Educator platform and paid pilots v2.5

**Outcome:** qualified educators can guide learners and validate the product at classroom scale.

- Add teacher workspaces, assignments, review queues, cohorts, and learner-controlled sharing.
- Create original-content authoring and quality-review workflows.
- Pilot with a small, diverse group of teachers and schools before broad institutional sales.
- Define organization roles, audit logs, retention policies, accessibility requirements, and support boundaries.
- Test paid learner and educator packages without changing the free-core covenant.

**Gate:** educator pilots demonstrate repeat use, manageable support demand, clear learning value, and no unresolved high-severity privacy or access-control findings.

### Stage E — Sustainable Pro v3.0

**Outcome:** revenue funds reliability, research, content review, accessibility, and community maintenance.

- Introduce clear Free, Pro, Educator, and Institution packages.
- Charge for hosted continuity, advanced audio computation, deeper adaptive analysis, collaboration, administration, and support.
- Add transparent limits, trials, cancellation, refunds, data export/deletion, regional pricing, and scholarship or sponsored-access paths.
- Track model and infrastructure cost per successful learning loop, not merely per request.
- Keep ads, sale of learner data, dark patterns, and fabricated urgency outside the business model.

**Gate:** paid retention and gross margin support continued service without reducing free-core quality or trust.

### Stage F — Open ecosystem v4.0

**Outcome:** third parties can extend the platform without fragmenting quality or learner safety.

- Release an extension SDK and compatibility suite.
- Launch a registry for verified adapters, original content packs, evaluators, and classroom integrations.
- Define review levels, provenance, version compatibility, permissions, security reporting, and removal rules.
- Support sustainable contributor and partner economics while keeping core governance independent.
- Publish migration policies so learners retain access to their data and plans when providers change.

**Gate:** extensions can be discovered, verified, upgraded, and revoked through transparent policies and automated compatibility checks.

### Stage G — Global reference ecosystem

**Outcome:** the project becomes a credible public reference for independent TOEFL-aligned learning quality.

- Maintain a multilingual teacher and learner council across regions and accessibility needs.
- Collaborate with researchers on public, privacy-respecting learning studies.
- Publish open evaluation protocols, longitudinal limitations, annual transparency reports, and format-change response history.
- Build educator certification for the project's methods without implying ETS authorization.
- Develop scholarships, nonprofit partnerships, and low-bandwidth access so global growth does not make essential learning less accessible.

**Gate:** leadership is demonstrated through reproducible learning evidence, community resilience, and trust—not marketing claims or repository stars alone.

## Free and paid covenant

| Capability | Open/free core | Hosted free | Paid later |
|---|---|---|---|
| All task-type coaching contracts | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Basic original practice and feedback | Yes | Yes, with fair limits | Higher limits and advanced workflows |
| Manual learner-state export/import | Yes | Yes | Automated sync and richer history |
| Public evaluations and source audits | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Advanced managed audio processing | Host-dependent | Limited trial | Higher limits and deeper analysis |
| Adaptive long-term planning | Portable recommendations | Basic | Advanced, synchronized, calendar-aware |
| Full mock orchestration | Manual/core components | Limited | Managed sessions and longitudinal comparison |
| Teacher classrooms and administration | Community tools may exist | No | Educator and institution plans |
| Priority support and service guarantees | Community | Community | Paid plans |

Pricing is deliberately deferred until real usage, retention, learning value, cost-to-serve, and willingness-to-pay have been measured. The product should not invent price points from competitors before it knows its own value and costs.

## Measurement system

### North-star metric

**Weekly learners completing an evidence-backed improvement loop:** distinct consenting learners who complete `attempt → evidence-based feedback → targeted retry → outcome check` within seven days.

This is stronger than downloads, messages, or GitHub stars because it represents delivered learning behavior. It must be paired with trust guardrails and cannot by itself prove long-term language improvement.

### Primary outcomes

1. **Verified retry improvement rate:** completed retries showing improvement on the explicitly targeted observable criterion, divided by comparable completed retries.
2. **Four-week retained learning-loop rate:** learners who complete a loop in week 1 and again in week 4, divided by eligible week-1 learners.
3. **Educator-confirmed usefulness rate:** sampled feedback sessions rated evidence-based and actionable by qualified reviewers.

### Drivers

- First-session activation: learners who complete their first full loop.
- Time to first useful feedback.
- Study-plan continuation and scheduled-review completion.
- Diagnostic coverage by section, task, and modality.
- Installation success and cross-platform compatibility.
- Contributor onboarding, review turnaround, and accepted high-quality contributions.

### Trust and business guardrails

- Unsupported official-score claims, current-format factual errors, protected-content incidents, and critical privacy/security incidents.
- Accessibility regressions and performance gaps by modality, language, or accent group.
- User-reported harmful or misleading feedback.
- Cost per completed learning loop, paid retention, refund rate, support burden, and gross margin once a hosted product exists.
- Free-tier completion and retention must not deteriorate merely to increase conversion.

### Target policy

Do not set growth or conversion targets without a baseline. Instrument consented events, run a documented pilot, and collect at least one complete retention window first. Release and trust gates may use absolute targets immediately—for example, zero unresolved critical findings and full passing status for maintained compatibility suites.

## Growth loops

- **Learning loop:** useful retry → visible progress → continued practice.
- **Teacher loop:** teacher review → better calibration → greater trust → more teacher adoption.
- **Contributor loop:** clear issue → verified contribution → public recognition → stronger coverage.
- **Localization loop:** reviewed terminology → local adoption → native-speaker feedback → improved localization.
- **Research loop:** transparent benchmark → external scrutiny → stronger evaluation → credible evidence.

## Non-negotiable trust rules

- Keep the public brand independent and clearly unaffiliated with ETS.
- Use original practice and link to official information instead of copying protected materials.
- Keep score estimates explicitly unofficial and evidence-bounded.
- Preserve a useful no-account and no-persistence path.
- Require explicit consent for analytics, cloud sync, audio retention, and educator sharing.
- Define the service's age scope before accounts launch and apply the relevant child-privacy safeguards.
- Let users export and delete hosted learner data through understandable controls.
- Publish material evaluation limitations and regressions.
- Keep extension provenance and verification levels visible.

## Decisions still needed

Recommended defaults are included so work can continue before each decision is final.

1. **First adoption wedge:** recommend independent learners plus a small teacher advisory group; institutions come after evidence and operational maturity.
2. **Launch languages:** recommend English as the canonical product language and Persian as the first fully reviewed explanation/localization path, followed by languages selected from measured demand.
3. **Commercial model:** recommend an open Apache-licensed core plus a paid hosted service; avoid a source-available license change unless governance and community impact are explicitly reviewed.
4. **Company and governance:** keep project governance transparent before accepting institutional data or marketplace revenue; choose the legal entity when paid pilots become real.
5. **Telemetry:** recommend no analytics in the portable core and opt-in, minimized product analytics in the hosted service.
6. **Marketplace economics:** decide revenue share only after extension quality, moderation cost, and liability are understood.
