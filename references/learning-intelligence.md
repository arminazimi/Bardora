# Diagnostics, study plans, and progress

Read this reference for diagnostic assembly, study-plan generation, spaced-review recommendations, progress summaries, or continuation from prior evidence. Read [portable learner state](learner-state.md) as well only when the learner requests persistence, export, import, or deletion.

## Diagnostic assembly

1. Confirm the learner's goal, available time, available modalities, and whether the request is a quick check, section diagnostic, or broad diagnostic.
2. Build the smallest set of original tasks that can answer the learner's question. Sample every section before making broad four-section claims; mark untested task types explicitly.
3. Record each observation by stable task ID, modality, evidence, confidence, strength, and no more than three priorities.
4. Separate observed performance from inference. A transcript does not establish audio delivery; one task does not establish an official section or total score.
5. End with an evidence-coverage table and identify the most valuable next probe when coverage is incomplete.

Useful diagnostic scopes:

- **Quick check:** a few targeted probes for one stated concern; produces a narrow plan and no overall inference.
- **Section diagnostic:** representative tasks from one section; identifies covered and untested task types.
- **Broad diagnostic:** samples Reading, Listening, Writing, and Speaking across one or more sessions; reports section-level patterns with modality limits.
- **Task inventory:** covers all 12 task types over multiple sessions when the learner wants comprehensive evidence.

## Study-plan contract

Build the plan from observed evidence, the learner's goal, and actual weekly capacity.

- Select one to three priorities and explain why they outrank other gaps.
- Give each activity a stable task ID, duration, focus, method, and observable success check.
- Allocate more practice to high-impact weak evidence while retaining light practice for demonstrated strengths.
- Match the schedule to the learner's available modalities; provide transcript or text alternatives without claiming equivalent audio measurement.
- Include a checkpoint that can change the plan when new evidence contradicts the current diagnosis.
- When evidence is sparse, label the first plan block as diagnostic and provisional.

Do not promise a score increase or admission outcome. Numeric results remain practice estimates under the shared feedback contract.

## Spaced-review recommendations

Schedule skills or error patterns, not copied test items. Use original isomorphic practice at each review.

- After a new or failed target, recommend a near-term retry and an initial review in roughly 1–3 days.
- After a clearly successful review, extend the interval progressively, for example toward 3, 7, and 14 days.
- After partial success, keep or modestly increase the interval and narrow the next drill.
- After a repeated failure, return to a near-term interval, reduce task complexity, and reteach the underlying cue.
- Adjust every interval to the learner's real schedule and target date. Use relative timing when no timezone or calendar date is available.

The interval is a recommendation, not a notification or background job. Create reminders only when the learner separately requests them and the host supports that action.

## Progress summaries

Summarize change over comparable evidence rather than activity volume alone:

1. scope and dates covered;
2. completed activities and modalities;
3. strengths newly demonstrated or sustained, with evidence;
4. priorities that improved, remained uncertain, or regressed;
5. coverage gaps and confidence limits;
6. the next focus and scheduled review.

Use `insufficient comparable evidence` when tasks, difficulty, modality, or source completeness differ too much to support a trend. Never turn completion counts into an official TOEFL score.

## No-persistence fallback

All diagnostics and plans must work without a state file. At the end of the session, provide a compact copyable handoff containing:

- current goal and time budget;
- evidence-covered task IDs;
- one demonstrated strength;
- up to three priorities;
- the next three activities and the first review interval;
- important limitations.

Tell the learner that continuity requires bringing this handoff back; do not claim the agent will remember it.

## Completion criterion

The learner has a plan whose priorities trace to evidence, every activity has a success check, review timing can adapt to results, and persistence status is explicit.
