# Complete the Words

Use this module for `reading.complete-the-words` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The complete passage or sentence context as displayed.
- Every incomplete word with its visible letters preserved.
- The learner's completion for each target.
- Any stated difficulty target or input constraint.

When the passage, cues, or learner completions are missing, request them before judging correctness.

## Success dimensions

1. **Cue fit:** the completion preserves the displayed letters and produces one word in the target position.
2. **Form:** spelling and morphology fit the local grammar.
3. **Meaning:** the word fits the sentence's meaning and natural collocation.
4. **Coherence:** the completed text remains consistent across sentences.

Accept a defensible alternative when it fits the visible cue, grammar, meaning, and wider passage. If the item permits multiple answers unintentionally, label it ambiguous and replace it rather than penalizing the learner.

## High-impact failure modes

- The word fits the topic but conflicts with the visible letters.
- The base word is plausible but its tense, number, or derived form is wrong.
- A locally grammatical word breaks the passage's meaning or reference chain.
- A generated gap has more than one equally natural completion.
- Feedback reveals every answer before the learner attempts the text.

## Original-practice contract

- Write the passage from scratch and avoid copying or closely paraphrasing ETS material.
- Keep one coherent everyday or academic context. The official Reading specification permits texts from roughly 15 to 200 words; choose a shorter passage for focused practice.
- In a text-only simulation, preserve a stable beginning cue for each target, such as `env_____`, and state that each gap takes one word.
- Use 4–8 targets for a short drill and mix lexical, morphological, and syntactic evidence at the stated difficulty.
- Privately restore every target and read the whole passage to confirm grammar and coherence.
- Prefer gaps with one clearly best answer. Replace ambiguous targets before presenting the exercise.
- Withhold the answer key until the learner attempts the targets.

## Coaching sequence

1. State that the task tests spelling and word-form knowledge plus contextual inference.
2. Teach one compact cue: visible letters first, then grammar, then passage meaning.
3. Collect the learner's completions.
4. Check each response against the visible cue and the full sentence.
5. Explain the highest-impact error with the smallest sufficient rule.
6. Give one new item that isolates the same cue as the retry.

## Feedback boundary

Report item-level practice results and error patterns. Do not convert a short drill into an official Reading section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner can explain which cue determined the answer and complete one targeted retry without seeing its answer key first.
