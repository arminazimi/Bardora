# Read in Daily Life

Use this module for `reading.read-in-daily-life` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The complete everyday text, including headings, labels, layout cues, and any linked text.
- The question and all response options when applicable.
- The learner's answer and, for diagnosis, the phrase that supported it.
- Any stated accessibility representation or difficulty target.

Judge accuracy only from source text that is available. When a meaningful layout cue is missing, state the limitation before evaluating it.

## Success dimensions

1. **Purpose:** identify why the communication exists and what action it invites.
2. **Detail:** locate names, dates, conditions, exceptions, and other explicit facts.
3. **Inference:** combine source clues without importing outside assumptions.
4. **Format navigation:** interpret prose and compact formats such as headings, lists, schedules, menus, or notices.

Require the learner to point to a source phrase during coaching so a correct guess can be distinguished from evidence-based reading.

## High-impact failure modes

- Choosing an option that matches the topic but not the communication's purpose.
- Missing an exception, deadline, condition, or changed detail.
- Treating a plausible real-world assumption as if the text stated it.
- Reading one cell, heading, or bullet without its label or neighboring context.
- Making essential evidence available only through color, position, or an undescribed image.

## Original-practice contract

- Create a new sign, message, email, schedule, menu, social post, or similar everyday text; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Keep the scenario culturally neutral and self-contained. Avoid brand knowledge, local conventions, or specialist facts needed to answer.
- Use a compact text appropriate to the target level. ETS describes easier Reading texts as typically 15–50 words and permits Read in Daily Life sets with two or three items.
- In each set, cover two or three distinct targets from purpose, detail, format navigation, or inference.
- Write one clearly supported answer per question. Make distractors traceable to a specific misreading rather than merely absurd.
- Provide a linear plain-text equivalent for tables, menus, or spatial layouts and name any visual relationship that matters.
- Withhold answers and explanations until the learner attempts the set.

## Coaching sequence

1. Name the text type and ask the learner to predict its purpose from the heading or opening.
2. Collect answers plus one supporting phrase for each.
3. Separate text evidence from the learner's inference.
4. Explain the smallest missed cue: purpose, qualifier, label, reference, or implied consequence.
5. Give one new everyday text that isolates the same cue.

## Feedback boundary

Report item-level practice results and the observed reading process. Do not convert one set into an official Reading section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner answers one targeted retry and cites the exact source cue that supports the answer.
