# Take an Interview

Use this module for `speaking.take-an-interview` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The academic or campus scenario and the complete sequence of interview questions.
- The learner's response to each question, with audio when delivery is evaluated.
- Transcripts or dependable notes for content, organization, vocabulary, and grammar evidence.
- Any recording, timing, accessibility, or missing-context limitation that affected performance.

Name the available modality before evaluating. A transcript supports content and language analysis, but it cannot establish pronunciation, intelligibility, pace, hesitation, rhythm, stress, intonation, or other prosodic evidence.

## Success dimensions

1. **Task fulfillment:** answer the exact question and remain relevant to the interview context.
2. **Content and elaboration:** provide a clear main response with specific, logically connected support rather than a list of undeveloped claims.
3. **Organization and coherence:** connect the answer, reason, example, and implication so a listener can follow the response easily.
4. **Language use:** use sufficiently precise vocabulary and controlled grammar to express the intended meaning; reward effective range rather than unnecessary complexity.
5. **Delivery:** maintain intelligible, reasonably continuous speech with prosody that supports meaning; evaluate only from learner audio and do not penalize accent difference by itself.

## High-impact failure modes

- Giving a polished response that does not answer the specific question.
- Stating an opinion without a reason, example, explanation, or relevant detail.
- Adding memorized material that does not connect to the interview scenario.
- Linking ideas ambiguously or using complex language that obscures the intended meaning.
- Treating ordinary planning pauses or an intelligible accent as automatic evidence of weak delivery.
- Claiming pronunciation, fluency, or prosody evidence from a transcript.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every scenario and question from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Use a coherent academic or campus-life situation that is accessible without specialized background knowledge.
- For a full set, create four related questions spoken by a simulated interviewer. Move from factual or personal experience toward explanation, comparison, or a supported opinion.
- Make each question answerable independently while maintaining a shared context across the set.
- Avoid sensitive personal disclosure, culture-specific assumptions, and questions with one expected opinion.
- Ensure the sequence elicits spontaneous language, relevant support, and connected speech rather than memorized facts.
- Present one question at a time and withhold model answers until the learner attempts it.
- Label generated questions, interviewer audio, and sample answers as original practice rather than official ETS content.

## Audio and accessibility paths

- **Question audio and learner audio available:** evaluate task fulfillment, content, organization, language, intelligibility, continuity, and meaningful prosody. Cite response language or timestamps.
- **Learner audio plus written questions:** evaluate the response and delivery while stating that comprehension of a spoken interviewer was not tested.
- **Transcript-only response:** evaluate task fulfillment, content, organization, vocabulary, and observable grammar. State that pronunciation, intelligibility, pace, hesitation, rhythm, stress, and intonation are untested.
- **Question audio inaccessible:** provide a written question or approved alternate representation and label the activity as accessible interview practice rather than an audio-comprehension diagnostic.
- **Missing question or scenario:** do not judge relevance or task fulfillment beyond the visible evidence; request the missing source or offer a fresh original set.

## Coaching sequence

1. Restate the question's communicative job in a short phrase, such as describe, explain, compare, or support an opinion.
2. Identify the learner's direct answer and the strongest supporting detail; note any required element that is missing.
3. Diagnose no more than three priorities across task fulfillment, support, organization, language, and audio-supported delivery.
4. Teach one compact answer pattern suited to the question, such as answer → reason → example → implication, without imposing a memorized script.
5. Ask for a short revision or a new response to one isomorphic interview question.

## Feedback boundary

Prefer qualitative feedback. An optional item-level **practice estimate** on the public 0–5 task scale requires the complete question and learner audio, must state its evidence and uncertainty, and must say that it is not an official ETS score. Do not convert four practice answers into an official Speaking section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner can identify the response's main strength and highest-impact gap, understands the evidence, and completes or receives one bounded interview retry.
