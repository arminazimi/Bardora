# Listen to a Conversation

Use this module for `listening.listen-to-a-conversation` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The audio when listening ability is being evaluated, or the complete transcript for language-only practice.
- The question and all response options when applicable.
- The learner's answer and any notes or recalled cues.
- Audible evidence relevant to attitude or intent, such as emphasis, hesitation, or intonation.

Label transcript-only work as comprehension or pragmatic-language practice. It cannot establish recognition of sounds, pacing, speaker changes, or prosody.

## Success dimensions

1. **Purpose and context:** identify who is speaking, why they are talking, and the immediate situation.
2. **Details:** retain explicit facts, constraints, changes, and next steps.
3. **Attitude and intent:** combine wording with audible delivery when audio is available.
4. **Inference:** predict or conclude only what the exchange strongly supports.
5. **Speaker tracking:** attribute statements and decisions to the correct speaker.

## High-impact failure modes

- Remembering a mentioned detail but missing that it was corrected or rejected later.
- Assigning one speaker's plan or opinion to the other speaker.
- Treating a polite phrase literally while missing its conversational intent.
- Inferring attitude from a transcript when the claim depends on tone.
- Writing questions that require unstated campus customs or outside knowledge.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every dialogue and question from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Use two speakers in a self-contained everyday social situation. Keep roles, problem, and outcome clear without relying on a named institution.
- For a focused drill, use a natural intermediate-length exchange; ETS describes intermediate listening input as typically 35–100 words.
- Include one meaningful turn, correction, constraint, or decision that supports questions about purpose, detail, attitude, or inference.
- Create one clearly best answer per question. Map correct answers to audible or textual evidence and distractors to a named misreading.
- Read the dialogue aloud to check speaker distinction, natural pacing, and whether delivery accidentally reveals an answer.
- Withhold the transcript during exam-like audio practice and withhold the answer key until the learner responds.

## Audio and accessibility paths

- **Audio available:** use distinct speakers, natural pacing, and one play for exam-like practice; retain the transcript for feedback.
- **Transcript-only:** show speaker labels and mark the activity as language/comprehension practice rather than an audio diagnostic.
- **Audio inaccessible:** provide a speaker-labeled transcript or approved alternate representation and name the missing acoustic evidence.
- Keep questions and options screen-reader compatible. Never make speaker identity available only through stereo position, color, or an image.

## Coaching sequence

1. Ask the learner to identify the situation and each speaker's goal.
2. Collect answers before showing the transcript or key.
3. Locate the decisive turn and identify whether the miss involved detail, correction, speaker, attitude, or inference.
4. Replay or quote only the smallest useful segment after the attempt.
5. Give one new exchange that isolates the same listening decision.

## Feedback boundary

State whether evidence came from audio or transcript. Report item-level practice results and avoid inferring an official Listening section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner identifies the decisive speaker turn and answers one targeted retry using the available modality.
