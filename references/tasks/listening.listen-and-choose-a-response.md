# Listen and Choose a Response

Use this module for `listening.listen-and-choose-a-response` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The audio stimulus when listening ability is being evaluated, or a transcript for language-only practice.
- The complete set of response options.
- The learner's selected response.
- Any available delivery evidence, such as emphasis, intonation, or audible correction.

Label transcript-only work as language and pragmatic practice. It does not demonstrate audio comprehension.

## Success dimensions

1. **Meaning:** understand the literal content of the brief utterance.
2. **Function:** identify whether it acts as a request, suggestion, offer, correction, question, refusal, or other conversational move.
3. **Continuity:** choose the response that naturally advances the exchange.
4. **Audio evidence:** use audible grammar, emphasis, and intonation when audio is available.

## High-impact failure modes

- Choosing a response that repeats keywords but answers the wrong function.
- Missing a negative, modal, pronoun, time reference, or correction.
- Selecting a grammatical response that is pragmatically unnatural.
- Claiming listening performance from a written transcript.
- Creating two options that are equally reasonable in the imagined context.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every utterance and option from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Use a brief, natural single exchange in an everyday social context. Keep the target within the official A1–B2 range and vary function and grammar across a set.
- Provide one response that clearly fits the utterance's meaning, function, and conversational next step.
- Make distractors plausible but diagnose different errors: keyword match, wrong function, polarity or reference error, or unnatural continuity.
- Read each exchange aloud before use. Replace items whose intended response depends on unstated context or for which two options remain defensible.
- Randomize the correct-option position across a set and withhold the key until the learner answers.

## Audio and accessibility paths

- **Audio available:** play or generate the utterance once for exam-like practice, keep options readable, and retain the transcript privately until feedback.
- **Transcript-only:** show the utterance, label the exercise as language/pragmatic practice, and avoid claims about listening accuracy.
- **Audio inaccessible:** provide the transcript or an approved alternate representation and report which listening evidence is unavailable.
- Keep response options compatible with screen readers and do not encode distinctions through sound effects or visual placement alone.

## Coaching sequence

1. Teach one cue: identify the speaker's function before comparing keywords.
2. Present the stimulus through the available modality and collect one choice.
3. Explain why the chosen response does or does not continue the exchange.
4. Name the distractor pattern without overloading the learner.
5. Give one new exchange targeting the same function.

## Feedback boundary

Report item-level practice results. State the modality used and do not convert a short drill into an official Listening section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner names the conversational function and selects a natural response in one targeted retry.
