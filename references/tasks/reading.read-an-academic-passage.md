# Read an Academic Passage

Use this module for `reading.read-an-academic-passage` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The complete passage with paragraph boundaries preserved.
- The question and all response options when applicable.
- The learner's answer and supporting passage evidence.
- The requested difficulty and diagnostic scope.

When the passage or question is incomplete, limit feedback to the evidence that remains visible.

## Success dimensions

1. **Key ideas:** distinguish the central claim or process from examples and minor facts.
2. **Details:** locate and connect explicit supporting information accurately.
3. **Organization:** recognize relationships such as cause–effect, comparison, sequence, problem–solution, and claim–evidence.
4. **Inference:** derive a necessary or strongly supported conclusion without exceeding the passage.
5. **Language in context:** resolve references and interpret vocabulary or phrases using local and discourse cues.

## High-impact failure modes

- Selecting a vivid detail as the main idea.
- Matching repeated words while ignoring the relationship expressed by the passage.
- Reversing cause and effect, chronology, or comparison direction.
- Treating a possible statement as a passage-supported inference.
- Using outside subject knowledge to override what the passage says.

## Original-practice contract

- Write a new, self-contained academic passage; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS, textbooks, abstracts, or news articles.
- Use a neutral explanatory topic that can be understood entirely from the passage. Treat factual claims as part of the constructed passage rather than requiring outside verification.
- Scale syntax, vocabulary, cohesion, and implicitness together. ETS describes Reading texts from short messages up to complex passages of about 200 words.
- Preserve paragraph boundaries and build a visible rhetorical structure.
- Create questions across key idea, detail, organization or relationship, and inference. Add vocabulary-in-context only when the passage supplies enough evidence.
- Ensure one clearly best answer. Every correct answer must map to passage evidence; every distractor must represent a plausible, named misreading.
- Read the passage and questions as a set before presenting them, then withhold the key until the learner attempts it.

## Coaching sequence

1. Ask for a one-sentence passage map: topic, main point, and paragraph roles.
2. Collect answers with a supporting line or relationship for each.
3. Classify an error as location, relationship, inference strength, reference, or vocabulary-in-context.
4. Contrast the chosen option with the best-supported option using passage evidence.
5. Give one shorter retry that isolates the same reading decision.

## Feedback boundary

Report the tested skills and item-level practice results. State when a short passage cannot represent the full Reading section; do not infer an official score.

## Completion criterion

The learner identifies the relevant passage relationship and answers one isomorphic retry using text evidence.
