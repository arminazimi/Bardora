# Listen to an Announcement

Use this module for `listening.listen-to-an-announcement` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The audio when listening ability is being evaluated, or the complete transcript for language-only practice.
- The question and all response options when applicable.
- The learner's answer and any notes or recalled cues.
- Any audible emphasis or discourse markers relevant to purpose and organization.

When only a transcript is available, evaluate content and organization without claiming audio comprehension.

## Success dimensions

1. **Purpose:** identify why the announcement is being made.
2. **Organization:** follow the setup, change or problem, instructions, and closing action.
3. **Details:** retain times, locations, conditions, exceptions, and changed information.
4. **Action and inference:** determine what listeners should do or what will probably happen next.
5. **Signal use:** recognize markers such as *because*, *instead*, *before*, and *finally*.

## High-impact failure modes

- Choosing a background detail as the announcement's main purpose.
- Remembering an original plan after the speaker replaces or cancels it.
- Missing a condition, exception, deadline, or required action.
- Using real-world assumptions instead of the announcement's stated information.
- Claiming that a transcript-based result measures recognition of spoken signals.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every announcement and question from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Use a self-contained classroom or campus-related situation without requiring knowledge of a real institution.
- For a focused drill, use natural intermediate-length input; ETS describes intermediate listening input as typically 35–100 words.
- Give the announcement a clear purpose and organization, including at least one actionable detail, condition, or change.
- Ask about purpose, organization, important detail, or supported next action. Provide one clearly best answer per question.
- Make distractors reflect a specific error: background-versus-purpose, old-versus-updated information, missed condition, or unsupported inference.
- Read the script aloud to verify clarity, natural pacing, and that vocal emphasis does not disclose the key.
- Withhold the transcript during exam-like audio practice and the answer key until the learner attempts the questions.

## Audio and accessibility paths

- **Audio available:** use clear natural delivery and one play for exam-like practice; preserve the transcript for feedback.
- **Transcript-only:** preserve paragraphing and signal words, and label the result as content/organization practice.
- **Audio inaccessible:** provide the transcript or an approved alternate representation and identify which acoustic evidence is unavailable.
- Keep questions and options screen-reader compatible. Encode changes and instructions in words, not sound effects or visual formatting alone.

## Coaching sequence

1. Ask for the purpose in one sentence before testing details.
2. Collect answers and any remembered signal words.
3. Reconstruct the announcement as purpose → change or reason → action.
4. Contrast the learner's choice with the decisive wording or audible cue.
5. Give one shorter announcement targeting the same failure mode.

## Feedback boundary

State the modality used and report item-level practice results. Do not convert one announcement into an official Listening section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner identifies the purpose, tracks the updated information, and answers one targeted retry.
