# Listen and Repeat

Use this module for `speaking.listen-and-repeat` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The reference audio and its exact transcript, or a clear statement that one is unavailable.
- The learner's response audio for pronunciation, intelligibility, pausing, rhythm, stress, or intonation feedback.
- A learner-response transcript, preferably checked against the recording, for word-level comparison.
- Item order and any playback, recording, or accessibility conditions that affected the attempt.

Name the evidence actually available before evaluating. A transcript of the learner's response can show additions, omissions, substitutions, and grammar changes, but it cannot establish pronunciation, intelligibility, pace, hesitation, rhythm, stress, or intonation.

## Success dimensions

1. **Repetition accuracy:** preserve the reference sentence's words, grammar, relationships, and meaning without additions, omissions, or substitutions.
2. **Intelligibility:** make the repeated sentence understandable with reasonable listener effort; evaluate this only from learner audio.
3. **Continuity:** sustain the sentence without breakdowns that obscure words or relationships; evaluate pauses and restarts only from learner audio.
4. **Meaningful prosody:** use segmentation, stress, rhythm, and intonation that support meaning; evaluate this only from learner audio and avoid treating accent difference as error.

## High-impact failure modes

- Preserving the general topic while changing a negation, number, condition, time, or relationship.
- Omitting function words or endings that change the sentence's grammar or meaning.
- Adding a plausible word that was absent from the reference.
- Breaking a long sentence into fragments that make relationships difficult to recover.
- Inferring pronunciation or fluency from a transcript, or penalizing an intelligible accent.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every scenario and sentence from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Use a self-contained academic or campus-navigation scenario that requires no specialized knowledge.
- For a full set, create seven related sentences that progress from short/simple to longer or syntactically more complex forms. For a targeted retry, one sentence is enough.
- Keep each item natural when spoken and give it one unambiguous reference transcript.
- Vary the memory load through length, clause structure, modifiers, numbers, negation, or conditions rather than obscure vocabulary.
- Review the set for cultural accessibility and for phrases that speech synthesis or a human speaker could deliver clearly.
- Present or play the reference once in exam-like practice, then collect one spoken repetition. Do not reveal the transcript before the attempt unless it is an accommodation or language-only drill.
- Preserve the transcript for feedback and label synthesized or coach-recorded audio as original practice, not official ETS audio.

## Audio and accessibility paths

- **Reference audio and learner audio available:** compare accuracy against the transcript and evaluate intelligibility, continuity, and meaningful prosody from the recording. Cite a word, phrase, or timestamp for each diagnosis.
- **Learner audio plus written reference only:** evaluate repetition accuracy and audible delivery, while stating that listening from spoken input was not tested.
- **Learner transcript only:** evaluate word and grammar correspondence only. State that pronunciation, intelligibility, pace, hesitation, rhythm, stress, and intonation are untested.
- **Reference audio inaccessible:** provide the transcript or an approved alternate representation and relabel the activity as read-and-repeat or language-form practice.
- **No learner response audio or transcript:** do not score the attempt; request an accessible response format or offer a new practice item.

## Coaching sequence

1. State the modality and whether the attempt tests listening, spoken delivery, both, or neither.
2. Compare the response with the reference by meaning-bearing chunks, then identify the highest-impact difference.
3. If audio exists, locate one delivery feature that affects intelligibility; otherwise keep delivery explicitly unassessed.
4. Teach one chunking, shadowing, or reconstruction technique tied to the observed error.
5. Give one new sentence with the same linguistic challenge and ask for a single spoken retry.

## Feedback boundary

Prefer qualitative feedback. An optional item-level **practice estimate** on the public 0–5 task scale requires both the complete reference and learner audio, must name the missing evidence and uncertainty, and must state that it is not an official ETS score. Never infer a Speaking section score from one item or a transcript-only response.

## Completion criterion

The learner can name the accuracy or intelligibility target, sees the evidence for it, and completes or receives one bounded retry matched to that target.
