# Listen to an Academic Talk

Use this module for `listening.listen-to-an-academic-talk` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The audio when listening ability is being evaluated, or the complete transcript for language-only practice.
- The question and all response options when applicable.
- The learner's answer, notes, and recalled evidence.
- Audible organizational cues, emphasis, pauses, or stance when relevant.

Transcript-only work can test ideas and discourse structure but not real-time decoding, pacing, pronunciation recognition, or prosody.

## Success dimensions

1. **Key idea:** identify the talk's central explanation or claim.
2. **Organization:** follow relationships such as cause–effect, comparison, sequence, problem–solution, and claim–example.
3. **Details and examples:** understand why a supporting fact or example is included.
4. **Inference:** derive conclusions and speaker stance that the talk supports.
5. **Language in context:** interpret references and less-common or idiomatic wording from surrounding speech.

## High-impact failure modes

- Selecting a memorable example as the main idea.
- Recording isolated facts without their relationship or function.
- Reversing a comparison, causal direction, or sequence.
- Extending a qualified claim beyond the talk's evidence.
- Using outside subject knowledge to override the speaker's explanation.
- Treating transcript success as proof of real-time listening performance.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every talk and question from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS, lectures, textbooks, articles, or transcripts.
- Use a self-contained academic topic whose claims can be understood entirely from the talk.
- Create a visible spoken structure with a central idea, supporting relationships, and examples. ETS permits extended monologic input up to about 250 words.
- Scale vocabulary, syntax, density, implicitness, and delivery together for the target difficulty.
- Ask across key idea, organization, detail or example function, and inference. Add vocabulary-in-context only when the talk supplies enough evidence.
- Provide one clearly best answer per question. Map each correct answer to the script and each distractor to a specific misreading.
- Read the script aloud to verify natural spoken language, pacing, pronunciation, and transitions; revise prose that sounds written rather than spoken.
- Withhold the transcript during exam-like audio practice and withhold the key until the learner responds.

## Audio and accessibility paths

- **Audio available:** use natural pacing and one play for exam-like practice; retain a timestamped transcript for feedback.
- **Transcript-only:** preserve paragraph and speaker-cue structure, label the activity as discourse/comprehension practice, and avoid listening-score claims.
- **Audio inaccessible:** provide a structured transcript or approved alternate representation and report the unavailable acoustic evidence.
- Keep questions and options screen-reader compatible. Express examples and rhetorical relationships in language rather than images, sound effects, or layout alone.

## Coaching sequence

1. Ask for a compact map: topic → central idea → major relationships → example functions.
2. Collect answers and notes before revealing the transcript or key.
3. Classify the miss as key idea, relationship, detail function, inference strength, reference, or vocabulary-in-context.
4. Replay or quote the smallest segment that resolves the decision.
5. Give one shorter talk or segment targeting the same relationship.

## Feedback boundary

State the modality and diagnostic scope. Report item-level practice results without inferring an official Listening section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner maps the talk's central relationship and answers one isomorphic retry from the available modality.
