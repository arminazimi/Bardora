# Build a Sentence

Use this module for `writing.build-a-sentence` practice and feedback.

## Evidence required

- The complete set of provided chunks, preserving their wording.
- The learner's assembled sentence when evaluating an attempt.
- Any stated constraint on capitalization, punctuation, or chunk reuse.

When the chunks or response are missing, request them before evaluating correctness.

## Success dimensions

1. **Completeness:** every required chunk appears exactly once, with no added content unless the prompt permits it.
2. **Grammar:** clause order, subject–verb relationships, auxiliaries, subordination, embedded questions, and modifier attachment form a valid sentence.
3. **Meaning and naturalness:** the sentence preserves the intended relationships and reads naturally.

Accept a different order when it is grammatical, uses every chunk correctly, and preserves a plausible intended meaning. Treat capitalization and punctuation as secondary unless the prompt explicitly tests them.

## High-impact failure modes

- A chunk is omitted, repeated, split, or rewritten.
- A dependent clause is treated as a complete sentence or attached to the wrong clause.
- Subject, finite verb, auxiliary, or embedded-question order is broken.
- A modifier attaches to the wrong noun or verb and changes the intended meaning.
- A grammatically valid alternative is rejected only because its order differs from the expected answer.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every item from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Give 3–5 chunks per item and target one main relationship.
- Mix relationships across a set: reason, contrast, time, relative clauses, or embedded questions.
- Verify privately that the chunks produce at least one complete natural sentence and do not require adding words.
- Avoid accidental ambiguity; when more than one order is valid, accept each defensible construction.
- Withhold the complete answer until the learner attempts the item.

## Coaching sequence

1. Name the target relationship and teach one compact cue without solving the item.
2. Collect the learner's sentence.
3. Check the chunk inventory before judging grammar.
4. Point to the exact relationship that works or breaks.
5. After the attempt, show one corrected sentence and explain the smallest necessary change.
6. Give one new isomorphic item as the retry.

## Feedback boundary

Report qualitative practice feedback unless a validated task-specific scale exists. Do not convert performance on these items into an official section or total score.

## Completion criterion

The learner can explain the target relationship and successfully assemble one retry item without a revealed answer.
