# Write an Email

Use this module for `writing.write-an-email` preparation, practice, drafting, feedback, and revision.

## Evidence required

- The exact prompt, including recipient, relationship, purpose, and every requested bullet.
- The learner's response when reviewing or revising.
- Any interface requirement for a subject, greeting, closing, timer, or word limit.
- Time spent and word count when available; neither is required for language-only feedback.

When the prompt is missing, analyze observable language and email conventions, lower confidence, and avoid a definitive task-fulfillment judgment.

## Success dimensions

1. **Task completion:** every requested bullet or action is addressed with relevant content.
2. **Purpose and development:** the opening makes the purpose clear, and facts plus impact make the request understandable.
3. **Action:** the recipient can identify a specific, realistic next step.
4. **Organization:** the response follows a useful functional sequence: `purpose → facts/context → impact → requested action → polite close`.
5. **Audience and conventions:** register, politeness, greeting, and closing fit the relationship and interface.
6. **Language control:** grammar, syntax, vocabulary, and collocations communicate naturally and accurately.

Score the response holistically. These dimensions explain the judgment; they are not separate weighted subscores.

## High-impact failure modes

- A prompt bullet is missing, only implied, or replaced with generic formality.
- The purpose appears late or remains vague.
- The email describes a problem but gives no concrete impact or feasible request.
- Register does not fit the relationship, such as ceremonial language to a classmate or commands to an authority figure.
- Memorized openings, closings, or ornate vocabulary consume space without advancing the task.
- A rewrite changes the learner's facts or attainable voice instead of repairing the response.
- One response is converted directly into an official Writing section or total score.

## Original-practice contract

- Write every prompt and model from scratch; do not copy or closely paraphrase ETS material.
- Define a recipient, relationship, communicative purpose, and two or three concrete content requirements.
- Use a self-contained academic, workplace, housing, service, or community scenario that requires no private data or outside knowledge.
- Vary task families across a set: request action, give feedback, offer advice, invite or recommend, apologize, inquire, follow up, or navigate a constraint.
- Ensure the requirements can be covered in a concise email without invented specialist facts.
- Allow plausible neutral details only when the prompt permits them and they do not contradict supplied facts.
- Withhold a complete model during normal practice until the learner attempts the prompt.

## Prepare and practice

1. Extract the recipient, relationship, purpose, tone, task family, and every prompt bullet.
2. Convert the prompt into a checklist using the functional sequence.
3. Suggest one realistic request or solution and only the factual detail needed to support it.
4. Offer six to ten scenario-specific collocations or short frames and the one or two grammar controls most relevant to the scenario.
5. Give a sentence-level outline. Reveal a complete model only when requested, after an attempt, or when demonstration is the lesson goal.

Match the register:

- **Friendly:** direct warmth, contractions, and practical support for friends or close peers.
- **Neutral-professional:** concise courteous requests for classmates, staff, services, or familiar authority figures.
- **Formal:** precise, restrained language when hierarchy or institutional context warrants it.

## Review and revise

1. Count words when text is available and map every prompt bullet as complete, partial, or missing.
2. Classify each sentence as purpose, fact/context, impact, action, close, or nonfunctional filler.
3. Report one holistic `0–5` **practice estimate**, band position (`low`, `solid`, or `borderline`), confidence, and the decisive reason. State that it is not an official ETS score.
4. Select at most three fixes, ordered by likely impact. Separate meaning or task problems from polish issues.
5. Teach corrections as `original → correction → reason` and preserve the learner's facts and attainable voice.
6. Unless the learner requests score-only feedback, provide a compact toolkit: corrected opening, one actionable request, six to ten useful phrases, two grammar controls, and a revised email.
7. End with one measurable retry, such as covering every bullet in four functional sentences or rewriting one vague request.

## Useful language controls

- `Could you please + base verb` for a direct courteous request.
- `Would it be possible to + base verb?` for a neutral-professional option.
- `I would appreciate it if you could + base verb.`
- `Would you mind + gerund?`
- `apologize for + noun/gerund`; use `because + clause` and `because of/due to + noun phrase`.
- Use past simple for completed events, present perfect for continuing effects, and future forms for plans or commitments.
- A subject line is optional unless the prompt or interface requires one.

## Feedback boundary

Use the public `0–5` task scale only as a labeled practice estimate. Never convert one email into an official `1–6` Writing section score or total TOEFL score. If the prompt is missing, state which task-completion judgments remain unavailable.

## Completion criterion

The learner can identify every prompt requirement, produce a relationship-appropriate actionable email, and complete one focused revision tied to the highest-impact gap.
